The legal foundation — FEMA

The Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), 1999, governs property ownership for NRIs and Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs). The Reserve Bank of India operationalises the rules.

What FEMA allows
  • Any residential property
  • Any commercial property — shops, offices, warehouses
  • Receiving property as gift or inheritance from Indian relatives
  • Renting out and earning rental income (taxable in India)
  • Selling property (with conditions on repatriation)
What FEMA prohibits
  • Buying agricultural land
  • Buying plantation property
  • Buying farmhouses

Inheritance of these from Indian relatives is allowed, but new purchase is not.

Funding your purchase — three legitimate channels

  1. NRE account. Funds remitted from abroad in foreign currency, converted and held in INR. Cleanest path. Principal AND interest are freely repatriable.
  2. NRO account. Indian-source income (rent from existing property, dividends, etc.). Repatriation limited to USD 1M/year after tax.
  3. FCNR account. Foreign-currency deposit held in India. Used when you want to retain currency-of-deposit. Repatriable.

Power of Attorney — the day-to-day enabler

You don't legally need a PoA to buy property. You can do everything yourself when you visit India. But realistically — site visits, document signing, registration, and dealing with bank queries can't always wait for your annual trip. A specific PoA to a trusted family member or lawyer in Bhopal removes that friction.

How to set up the PoA

  1. Draft a specific PoA (not general) — scope it to "purchase, register, and manage" property at a defined address. A general PoA is risky.
  2. Sign it at the Indian consulate/embassy nearest you. The consulate notarises and seals it.
  3. Send the original to your PoA holder in India.
  4. The PoA holder pays stamp duty (~₹500 in MP) and registers it at the Sub-Registrar's office in Bhopal.
  5. Registered PoA is then valid for all listed actions.

Taxes — what you'll actually owe

  • At purchase: Stamp duty + registration as per MP rates (7.5% standard, 6.5% for women buyers).
  • TDS on purchase: 1% of value if you're buying from another resident; varies if buying from another NRI.
  • Rental income: Taxable in India at slab rates. 30% standard deduction for maintenance is automatic.
  • Capital gains on sale: Short-term (≤2 years) at slab rates; long-term (>2 years) at 20% with indexation, or 12.5% without.
  • DTAA relief: Most countries (US, UK, UAE, Singapore, Canada, Australia) have Double Tax Avoidance Agreements — you don't pay tax on the same gain twice.

Repatriation — getting money back out

  • If funded from NRE account: Both principal and gains freely repatriable after applicable taxes.
  • If funded from NRO account or rental income: Up to USD 1 million per financial year per individual, after taxes paid + CA certificate (Form 15CA/CB).
  • If acquired before NRI status: Same USD 1M/year cap applies.

A specific suggestion if you're NRI buying for your parents

The most common NRI use case we see — buying a property for parents living in Bhopal or planning to retire there.

Register in the parent's name (using funds you remit to their account as gift — there's no tax on gifts to direct relatives in India). You save 1% stamp duty if it's the mother's name. You avoid the complications of an NRI registered owner managing a tenanted property from abroad. And when the property eventually transfers to you via inheritance, capital gains start from the original purchase date.
