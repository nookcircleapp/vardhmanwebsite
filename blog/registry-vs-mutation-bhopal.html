Due Diligence · Land Records

Registry is not ownership. Mutation is.

Why your name has to reach the government's land records after the sale deed is registered, how namantaran works in Madhya Pradesh, and what goes wrong when buyers skip it.

8 min read· Oct 2026· Vardhman Constructions·
  • Registry proves the sale. The registered sale deed records a contract between seller and buyer. It does not by itself update the government's land records.
  • Mutation updates the record. Called namantaran in MP and dakhil-kharij in many states, it replaces the seller's name with yours in the revenue or municipal records.
  • Skipping it is risky. While the old owner still appears in the records, the land can be resold, mortgaged or tangled in disputes that take years to clear.
  • Banks ask for it. Home loans, loans against property and building permissions all need an updated record, not just the sale deed.
  • Do it right after registry. Apply within days of registration and keep certified copies of the updated record.

Most buyers treat the day the sale deed is registered as the finish line. The stamp duty is paid, the photographs are taken at the Sub-Registrar's office, and the original deed comes home in a file.

It feels final. Legally, it is only half the job. In our three decades of building in Bhopal, the most painful disputes we have seen rarely came from a bad sale deed. They came from what buyers did not do after it.

Registry and mutation are two different things

Two separate government systems are involved when you buy land or a home, and they do not automatically know about each other.

StepWhat it doesWhere it happens
RegistryRegisters the sale deed, the contract that proves the seller transferred the property to you for a price.Sub-Registrar office, through the Sampada portal in MP.
MutationChanges the owner's name in the government's land or property records, so the state recognises you as the holder.Tehsil (revenue land) or the municipal corporation (urban property tax records).

Think of it this way: the registry is your proof of purchase, and mutation is the government acknowledging it. One without the other leaves your title incomplete.

What mutation actually changes

For agricultural and revenue land in Madhya Pradesh, the key records are the khasra (the plot-wise record) and the B-1 (the holder-wise record, similar to the khatauni or jamabandi in other states). Mutation removes the seller's name from the owner column and enters yours.

For flats and houses inside municipal limits, the equivalent step is getting the property transferred to your name in the Bhopal Municipal Corporation's property tax records. Many apartment buyers forget this entirely, then discover the problem years later when they try to sell or take a loan.

What goes wrong when you skip it

Real risks, not theory
  • Double sale. A dishonest seller who still appears as owner in the records can sell the same land again to someone who checks only the land records.
  • Hidden loans. The seller can use the old record to take a loan against land that is no longer theirs.
  • Long civil suits. Untangling competing claims in court can take the better part of a decade.
  • Stuck finance. Banks will not sanction a home loan, loan against property or construction finance on a sale deed alone.
  • Blocked approvals. Building permissions, diversion and many government schemes ask for the current land record in your name.

How mutation (namantaran) works in Madhya Pradesh

  1. Register the sale deed at the Sub-Registrar's office through Sampada.
  2. Apply for namantaran at the tehsil or online through the state's revenue services. In many cases, a registry that transfers a complete khasra is now routed automatically to MP's cyber tehsil system for mutation.
  3. Patwari report and public notice. The patwari verifies the transaction and a notice is issued so that anyone with a claim can object.
  4. Order by the Tehsildar. If no objection comes in, the mutation is approved and the record is updated.
  5. Collect the updated record. Download or obtain a certified copy of the new khasra and B-1 showing your name.

Undisputed mutations are usually settled within about a month. Disputed ones go to a hearing and can take much longer, which is exactly why you want to start the day the deed is registered.

Check it online

You can view khasra and B-1 records for MP land on the state's Bhulekh land records portal, mpbhulekh.gov.in. Search by district, tehsil, village and khasra number, and confirm your name appears as the holder.

Before you buy: check the seller's mutation too

Mutation matters before the sale as much as after it. This is especially true for inherited or ancestral land.

  • Confirm the seller's name is on the current record. If the land still shows a late grandparent or another relative, the seller cannot give you a clean title yet.
  • Ask the seller to complete their own mutation first. Only then pay the token amount and execute the sale. Shortcuts here are how most title disputes begin.
  • Match the record to the deed. Khasra number, area and owner name should agree across the land record, the previous sale deed and the deed you are signing.

Your post-registration checklist

  1. Collect the registered sale deed and keep the original safe.
  2. Apply for mutation at the tehsil, online, or confirm the cyber tehsil has picked it up.
  3. Track the application until the Tehsildar's order is passed.
  4. Get a certified copy of the updated khasra and B-1 in your name.
  5. For urban property, transfer the property tax account to your name with the municipal corporation.
  6. Pay property tax in your own name from the next cycle onward.
  7. Keep digital and paper copies of every document together.
The rule is simple. The registry is your legal agreement, and mutation is your recognition in the government's records. Neither is complete without the other. Frequently asked

Does registering the sale deed make me the owner?

The registered sale deed proves the seller transferred the property to you. To be recognised as owner in the government's land or property records, you also need mutation, which replaces the seller's name with yours.

What is mutation or namantaran?

Mutation, called namantaran in Madhya Pradesh and dakhil-kharij in several other states, is the process of updating the owner's name in the revenue records (khasra and B-1) or the municipal property tax records after a sale, inheritance or gift.

How long does mutation take in MP?

Undisputed mutations are usually settled within about a month of applying. If someone files an objection, the Tehsildar hears both sides, which can take considerably longer.

Can mutation be done online in Madhya Pradesh?

Yes. Many land transactions registered through Sampada that transfer a complete khasra are routed automatically to MP's cyber tehsil system. You can check the updated record on mpbhulekh.gov.in.

What happens if I don't get mutation done?

The seller continues to appear as owner in the records. That opens the door to the land being resold or mortgaged, makes it hard to get a home loan or building permission, and can lead to long civil disputes.

Do flat buyers need mutation too?

Yes. For a flat or house in Bhopal, transfer the property tax account into your name with the Bhopal Municipal Corporation after registration. Banks and future buyers will ask for it.

What should I check before buying inherited land?

Confirm the seller's own name is on the current khasra and B-1. If the record still shows a deceased relative, ask the seller to complete their mutation first, before you pay a token or sign the sale.
