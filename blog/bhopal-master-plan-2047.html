What was published, and when

On 25 September 2026 the Directorate of Town & Country Planning issued notification No. 101-T&CP-D.P.-2026. It publishes the Draft Bhopal Development Plan 2047 under Section 18(1) read with Section 23(2) of the Madhya Pradesh Nagar Tatha Gram Nivesh Adhiniyam, 1973. The notice is signed by Kaushlendra Vikram Singh, Commissioner-cum-Director.

This is the first Bhopal draft to reach the public since the 2031 draft was scrapped in February 2024. The long backstory of lapsed plans and cancelled drafts is further down this page.

For scale, the last notified plan, Bhopal Development Plan 2005, was published on 9 June 1995. It covered 601.06 sq km and 146 villages. The 2047 draft plans for a city roughly two-thirds larger by area.

The four pillars of the 2047 draft

The highlights booklet says the plan rests on four ideas:

  1. Conservation of the natural ecosystem, above all the Bhojtaal (Upper Lake) and its catchment, a Ramsar site.
  2. Reform-driven planning that simplifies rules instead of adding them.
  3. A modern, citizen-friendly approach to regulations and approvals.
  4. Self-sustaining growth that attracts investment and creates jobs.

17 headline proposals, in plain English

Growth and land use

  • A 51 km growth corridor. It links the proposed EKAI City, the airport, the proposed CMU, the logistics and wholesale zone and several suburbs. About 3,000 hectares are earmarked under the corridor for high-density mixed-use development, with a BRTS line that can later be upgraded to MRTS. The foreword says the eastern bypass has been identified as this growth corridor, and a new green bypass is proposed.
  • Unlimited FAR along the corridor and on wider roads. FAR is now set by road width. Plots on sufficiently wide roads are freed from FAR and height caps.
  • Road-width-linked mixed land use. Following the state's TOD policy, residential land on wider roads can take mixed uses so work and home sit closer together.
  • Transit Oriented Development zones where radial roads meet the growth corridor.
  • EKAI City at Bhauri. The existing education hub is to become an Education, Knowledge and Artificial Intelligence city.

Rules for building

  • Ground coverage limits removed. Setbacks (MOS) have been reworked so plots can be used more efficiently.
  • Simpler development norms written to be understood by ordinary citizens, not just consultants.
  • Creative redevelopment. Plot amalgamation and base-FAR relaxations encourage rebuilding in older areas. An RWA can redevelop a residential apartment block with 70% resident consent.
  • Affordable housing incentives. Affordable projects are exempt from buying extra FAR. Rental housing for migrant residents also gets incentives.
  • Parking reforms, including multilevel and mechanised parking, parking under open spaces and flyovers, RWA-managed paid street parking, and plot amalgamation for basement parking.
  • Private developers can build plan roads, with land provided through government land or TDR.

Green, heritage and city management

  • Green area of about 16.5%, above URDPFI guideline norms. A golf course is proposed to redevelop the Bhanpur khanti (old landfill).
  • Colony open space raised from 10% to 12%, plus a further 2% for service areas.
  • Green TDR and Heritage TDR compensate owners whose land falls in recreational or heritage zones.
  • Revenue for implementation from premium FAR, use-conversion fees and conservation charges.
  • Safe City provisions, including networking private CCTV in public spaces and transparent boundary walls on public buildings.
  • A Digital Twin City for real-time monitoring of implementation and unauthorised construction.

How to find your land on the map

  1. Download the land use map above. Open it in Adobe Acrobat Reader or a desktop browser. Phone viewers can struggle with a sheet this large.
  2. Press Ctrl+F and search your village name in capital letters, for example KOLUA, BHAURI or RATIBAD. The map text is searchable.
  3. Zoom in until khasra numbers appear. Find your survey number inside the village boundary.
  4. Match the colour against the legend on the right side of the sheet. Note the zone code and the width marked on the nearest proposed road, such as 18 M or 24 M.
  5. Look up that zone in the gazette's development regulations to see permitted uses, FAR and setbacks.
  6. Save a copy today. Draft maps get revised. The version you download now is your record of what was originally proposed for your land.
Check the khasra, not the locality

News reports talk about areas like Kolar or Ayodhya Bypass as a whole. Zoning on this map changes parcel by parcel. Two neighbouring khasras can fall in different zones, so always check your exact survey number.

How to file an objection or suggestion

Anyone can object to or suggest changes to the draft. The window is 30 days from the gazette date of 25 September 2026, which puts the deadline around 25 October 2026. Do not leave it to the last day.

  1. Write to the Joint Director, Town & Country Planning, District Office, Bhopal, or email obj-sugg-bhopal@mp.gov.in.
  2. Identify the land precisely. Give the village name, khasra number, area and your ownership details.
  3. State the current use and the proposed zone as shown on Map No. 14.1.
  4. Explain what you want changed and why. Stick to facts such as existing construction, approvals already granted, road access or errors in the base map.
  5. Attach supporting documents, such as the khasra copy, diversion order, building permission, or photographs.
  6. Keep proof of submission. Get an acknowledgement for a hand-delivered letter, and keep the sent email.
A draft is not the final plan

After the objection period, the authority hears the objections, may modify the plan, and the state government then approves and notifies the final version. The 2031 draft drew thousands of objections and was withdrawn entirely. Until the final plan is notified, treat every zone on this map as provisional.

Why this plan matters: the backstory

Ask any Bhopal buyer what they are waiting for and a surprising number will say the same thing: the master plan. They have read that a new plan is coming, that it will redraw land use across the city, and that it is smarter to hold until it lands.

We have been building in this city since 1992, and we have watched three generations of buyers wait for exactly that. Here is the honest picture, with dates, so you can decide what to do rather than what to hope for.

Why there is a 2047 plan at all: 21 years of drafts

To understand the 2047 plan you have to understand what it replaces. Bhopal's last notified development plan came into force in 1995 with a horizon year of 2005. It expired more than two decades ago. Everything since has been a draft.

1995–2005

The last plan that actually had legal force

The Bhopal Development Plan notified in 1995, valid to 2005. Every land use decision made since has been governed by a lapsed document, ad-hoc permissions, and local body discretion.

2009

Draft one — dropped

The first replacement attempt was prepared and then set aside over shortcomings in the survey and land use base.

2020

Draft two — cancelled

A draft prepared under the then Congress government. Cancelled after the change of government.

June 2023

Draft three — BDP 2031, published for objections

The most complete attempt yet: a planning area of 1,016.90 sq km covering 248 villages, expanded from the earlier 813.92 sq km by absorbing roughly 51 surrounding villages. It proposed eight sub-cities, a Transit Oriented Development corridor along the metro alignment, and strict construction control around the Upper Lake and Kerwa catchments. It drew close to 3,000 formal claims and objections — some reports put the count above 5,000 — heavily from farmers and elected representatives.

Feb 2024

BDP 2031 scrapped — the 2047 brief is issued

Urban Development Minister Kailash Vijayvargiya cancelled the 2031 draft and directed officials to prepare a fresh plan visualising the city of 2047 rather than 2031. A 74-member committee was constituted — the local MP, MLAs, the mayor, district officials, gram panchayat heads, architects, engineers and Institute of Town Planners India representatives — specifically so that elected representatives could not later claim they had been excluded.

2025

The Metropolitan Act changes the frame

The Madhya Pradesh Metropolitan Area Planning and Development Act 2025 is approved, creating metropolitan development authorities for cities above 10 lakh population, chaired by the Chief Minister. The Bhopal authority is to combine parts of Bhopal, Sehore, Raisen, Vidisha and Biaora.

Aug 2026

Master plans put on hold

The state confirmed the Bhopal and Indore master plans have been set aside for now because both cities are being developed as metropolitan areas. The Bhopal draft has been discussed repeatedly within the department but has not been presented to the Chief Minister, and no final draft had been prepared at that point.

25 Sep 2026

Draft BDP 2047 published for objections

The Directorate of Town & Country Planning notified the Draft Bhopal Development Plan 2047 in the MP Gazette: 1,016.90 sq km, 254 villages, 45 lakh projected population, with a 30-day window for objections. Download the documents.

The Upper Lake catchment — still the hardest line on the map

The draft handles the lake through graded Bhojtaal Influence Zones (BIZ0 to BIZ2) on the land use map. Check those bands first if you own or are buying land anywhere near the lake.

The objection that killed the last draft

The 2031 draft proposed expanding the Upper Lake (Bada Talab) catchment protection zone from roughly 2,800 hectares to 3,872 hectares. Farmers objected in large numbers — land inside the expanded catchment loses development value, and the proposal arrived without a compensation mechanism. Any 2047 plan has to solve this politically before it can be notified. If you are being offered land anywhere in the Kolar, Kerwa, Bhauri or Upper Lake catchment belt, this is the risk you are actually buying.

The metropolitan region — the thing most buyers are missing

While everyone watches the master plan, the more consequential change has already happened in law. Under the MP Metropolitan Area Planning and Development Act 2025, Bhopal is no longer being planned as a city. It is being planned as a region — combining parts of Bhopal, Sehore, Raisen, Vidisha and Biaora, chaired by the Chief Minister.

Why this matters for a home-buyer

A metropolitan boundary that reaches Sehore and Raisen changes which corridors count as "outskirts". The stretches connecting Bhopal to those districts — Hoshangabad Road toward Mandideep and Obaidullaganj, Ayodhya Bypass and the Vidisha side, the Sehore axis west — stop being edge locations and become internal links of a planned region. That is the same transition Hoshangabad Road went through between 2010 and 2018, when it moved from "far" to "central" and prices followed.

It also explains the hold-up. It is difficult to notify a 1,016 sq km city master plan in the same month you create a regional authority with a wider boundary and a different statutory basis. The government has effectively chosen to sequence the region first.

The Supreme Court angle: why the delay now has teeth

For twenty years the missing master plan was an administrative embarrassment rather than a practical problem. That changed when the Supreme Court took a firm view on commercial activity being run out of residential plots.

In a city with no notified plan, there is no clean, current, legally defensible map of what is commercial and what is residential. Shops and offices across Bhopal have operated inside residential-zoned buildings for years under the lapsed 1995 plan and local discretion. With the court restricting that, traders across the city are now pressing for a master plan precisely so mixed land use and commercial frontages can be formally designated. Reports in August 2026 suggest the state may bring separate legislative relief for traders rather than wait for the plan.

If you are buying to run a business from home

Do not assume a residential flat or plot can be used commercially "because everyone does it here". Until land use is formally notified, that assumption is legally exposed. If you need commercial use, buy on land that is already commercially designated — see our commercial property guide.

So should you wait for the plan before buying?

No. And we would say this even if it cost us a sale.

Run the arithmetic. A buyer who decided in 2009 to wait for the "new master plan" before buying in Bhopal has now waited seventeen years, through three cancelled drafts, while prices on the main residential corridors have multiplied several times over. Waiting has never been the safe option here. It has been the expensive one.

More importantly, the protections that actually matter to a home-buyer do not come from the master plan at all. They come from four things you can verify today:

  1. RERA registration. Check the project on rera.mp.gov.in — registration number, declared possession date, and complaint count. Our step-by-step method is in the RERA verification guide.
  2. Land diversion order. The land must be formally diverted from agricultural to residential use under the MP Land Revenue Code. Ask to see the diversion order, not a promise that it is "in process".
  3. Sanctioned building permission / colony approval from Bhopal Municipal Corporation, BDA, or the relevant local body — with an approved layout showing road widths and open space.
  4. Clear title chain and bank project approval. If two or more scheduled banks have approved the project, their legal teams have already audited the title at their own risk. That is free due diligence.

A project that clears all four is safe whether the plan lands in 2027 or 2032. A project that clears none of them is unsafe no matter what the plan eventually says.

Where the risk actually sits

The plan being pending does not create uniform risk across the city. It concentrates in specific places.

Higher exposure while the plan is unnotified
  • Undiverted agricultural land sold as plots in the newly absorbed village belt — the Sukhi Sevniya, Bangarsia, Bagrouda and Amla directions where the boundary was proposed to extend. A future green or catchment designation can freeze this land entirely.
  • Upper Lake, Kerwa and Kolar catchment fringes. The catchment boundary is the single most contested line in the plan. Buying inside a line that has not been finally drawn is speculation, not investment.
  • Residential plots marketed for commercial returns — directly exposed to the Supreme Court position described above.
  • Colonies with sub-18-metre internal roads. If the 18-metre norm carries into the notified plan, these become hard to regularise and hard to resell.
The lowest-risk position in an unnotified-plan city is boring and well-documented: a RERA-registered project, on diverted residential land, with a sanctioned layout, on an already-built arterial corridor, from a builder whose delivery record you can physically go and inspect. Both Vardhman Fairmont on Hoshangabad Road and Vardhman Celestia on Ayodhya Bypass sit on established, fully-serviced corridors inside the existing municipal envelope — not on speculative outer-belt agricultural land waiting for a designation.

How to track the plan yourself

  1. mptownplan.gov.in — the Directorate of Town & Country Planning. Draft plans, land use panels and the existing BDP documents are published here. The 2047 draft was published here on 25 September 2026.
  2. mpurban.gov.in — Urban Development & Housing Department notifications.
  3. The claims-and-objections window. When a draft is published, a statutory period opens for written objections. That window — not a news headline — is the moment the plan becomes real and the moment your survey number's designation becomes visible.
  4. Check the survey number, not the locality. When the draft does land, look up your specific khasra/survey number on the land use map. Locality-level reporting will tell you nothing about your plot.
One habit worth keeping

Whenever a draft plan is finally published, download the land use map PDF that same week. Draft maps get revised, replaced and occasionally withdrawn. The version you downloaded on day one is often the only evidence of what was originally proposed for your land.

Our read, as builders

We expect the 2047 plan — or whatever the metropolitan authority ultimately notifies in its place — to do three things: push the planning boundary well beyond today's municipal limits, tighten green and catchment protection significantly, and formalise mixed land use along major road frontages to resolve the commercial-use problem.

If that reading is right, the land that benefits most is land that is already serviced, already diverted, already on a wide arterial road, and already inside the built envelope. The land that suffers most is speculative outer-belt agricultural land bought on the assumption that a plan will bless it. That has been the pattern in every Indian city that has notified a long-delayed master plan, and there is no obvious reason Bhopal will be the exception.

Frequently asked

Where can I download the Bhopal Master Plan 2047 PDF?

In the download section at the top of this page: the full draft gazette, the land use map and the highlights booklet, with lite versions for mobile. The official source is mptownplan.gov.in/Scheme.aspx.

What is the Bhopal Master Plan 2047?

It is the development plan for Bhopal with a horizon year of 2047, replacing the scrapped Bhopal Development Plan 2031 draft. The state cancelled the 2031 draft in February 2024 and the Town & Country Planning department redrafted it around Bhopal's projected 2047 population.

Has the Bhopal Master Plan 2047 been released?

Yes, as a draft. Notification No. 101-T&CP-D.P.-2026 dated 25 September 2026 published the Draft Bhopal Development Plan 2047 for objections and suggestions. It is not yet the final approved plan.

What is the last date to file an objection?

Objections must be filed within 30 days of the gazette notice of 25 September 2026, so around 25 October 2026. Send them in writing to the Joint Director, T&CP District Office, Bhopal, or email obj-sugg-bhopal@mp.gov.in. See how to file an objection.

How big is the planning area?

1,016.90 sq km, including 254 villages, planned for an estimated 45 lakh people. The 2005 plan covered 601.06 sq km and 146 villages.

How do I find my land on the map?

Open the land use map PDF in Adobe Reader or a desktop browser, press Ctrl+F, and search your village name in capital letters. Zoom in to your khasra number and match its colour to the legend. Full steps are in how to find your land.

What are the key features of the draft?

A 51 km growth corridor with about 3,000 hectares, unlimited FAR and height on wider roads, no ground coverage limit, road-width-linked mixed use, about 16.5% green area, colony open space raised to 12%, Green and Heritage TDR, RWA redevelopment with 70% consent, EKAI City at Bhauri and a Digital Twin City.

Is the Bhopal Master Plan 2047 final?

No. Objections will be heard, the plan may be modified, and the state government must approve and notify the final version. Zoning on the draft can change before then.

Which master plan is legally in force in Bhopal right now?

No plan is currently notified. The plan notified in 1995 was valid to 2005 and has lapsed. The 2009, 2020 and 2023 drafts were never notified, and the 2047 draft is still at the objection stage.

Why was the Bhopal Master Plan 2031 cancelled?

It drew close to 3,000 formal objections, some reports say over 5,000, largely from farmers and elected representatives. The proposed expansion of the Upper Lake catchment from about 2,800 to 3,872 hectares was the biggest flashpoint. The Urban Development Minister cancelled it in February 2024 and ordered a fresh draft built around 2047.

How does the Bhopal Metropolitan Authority affect the master plan?

Under the MP Metropolitan Area Planning and Development Act 2025, Bhopal is being developed as a metropolitan area combining parts of Bhopal, Sehore, Raisen, Vidisha and Biaora, chaired by the Chief Minister. The draft is designed to align with that region, which may widen the planning boundary beyond municipal limits over time.

Should I wait for the master plan before buying a home in Bhopal?

No. Waiting has cost buyers far more in appreciation than it has saved in risk. Check your plot on the draft map, then buy RERA-registered projects on diverted residential land with sanctioned building permission, and avoid unapproved plots in lake catchment or green belt areas.
