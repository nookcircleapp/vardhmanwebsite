7 things to consider before buying a house in Bhopal.

A builder's checklist — written from three decades of work in this city, not from a brochure. Location, layout, amenities, paperwork, taxes, and home loans, in plain language.

Executive Summary

The short version.

Thinking about buying a house in Bhopal? It's a huge step, and this guide breaks down seven key things to keep in mind so you can make a smart choice.

First up, really think about the location. It's not just about being close to your job or the market now; you should also peek at the city's Master Plan to guess how the area might grow in the future. When you visit a potential colony, check out the layout — wide roads, ideally 25–30 feet, so you don't have to deal with traffic jams just to get home. Inside the house, look for smart designs with good airflow and natural light.

Before you get too attached, do your homework on the builder and the project's legal status. Most of the important documents — RERA approval and land titles — are on the MP RERA website. Don't just get wowed by the fancy clubhouse; make sure basics like water, power backup, and security are solid.

On the money side, remember you can get tax breaks on your principal and interest payments under the old tax regime. Getting a loan depends on your income and a CIBIL score over 700. Finally, ask about who handles maintenance after you move in, so there are no surprises down the road.

01
Chapter One

Township location & accessibility.

When purchasing a home, the location of the township and its proximity to your workplace, kirana shops, restaurants, malls, hospitals and sports turfs are some obvious factors to consider.

Moreover, buying a home with your life-long savings is a high-risk investment — and you should be rewarded for that risk with higher returns. The overall development of the area and its proximity to main roads will dictate the future property prices of the locality.

One way to check this is by looking at the existing Master Plan of the city and taking an educated guess about the future prospects of that area.

How to access the Bhopal Master Plan

Go to mptownplan.gov.in/plan_bhopal.html — the Directorate of Town & Country Planning, Madhya Pradesh, publishes layouts, panel maps and development plans here.

02
Chapter Two

Design, layout, and density.

2.1 — Colony Layout

When assessing the layout of a colony, look for open spaces, green areas, the width of colony roads, and recreational areas. A well-planned township should feel spacious — not overly dense. The ideal colony road size is 25–30 ft, because you have to account for car parking on both the right- and left-hand side of the road. Anything less and you'll have trouble navigating the township.

2.2 — Floor Plan

2.2.1 For Duplexes / Triplexes

Most people look at the sizes of bedrooms and the kitchen, but home design should foster relationships and communication within a family. Look at the layout with that lens. Inter-floor connectivity is key — a home should feel connected from all areas, through the use of double-height ceilings, wide staircases, and open layouts in the common areas.

2.2.2 For Flats

Good architecture considers ventilation and sunlight access in every room. Dark rooms are depressing. You are buying a place you will call home — natural light is necessary for your family's mental wellbeing.

2.2.3 Space Utilization

See how the developer has designed the home to understand how interior spaces are efficiently utilised. Are there awkward corners, unusable spaces, or long dark lobbies at the entrance?

2.2.4 Privacy

Pay special attention to the placement of windows, doors and balconies — especially if your home is a flat in a multi-storey building. High-density projects, busy localities and closeness to railway stations might increase property prices, but they can be quite noisy. This trade-off can be mitigated by using high-quality windows that provide insulation. Ask your developer about the details of the windows and doors.

Another important issue: does the township have efficient rainwater drainage? Visit the colony during the monsoon to understand the slope of the land and where excess water flows.

2.2.5 Vastu

Open any brochure a developer gives you and they all say their colony is Vastu compliant. Honestly, it's very rare that every single unit can be Vastu compliant. Developers have space and design constraints and have to face trade-offs. In one multi-storey building you will have both east- and west-facing flats. In residential colonies you will have both sher mukhi ghar and gow mukhi ghar, because not all plots are in the perfect shape. Keeping this in mind, look at whether the design of the flat/house doesn't contradict the Vastu rules of architecture.

That said, good developers put real effort into following Vastu norms as much as is practically possible. If you are concerned about Vastu compliance, buy early while the project is under construction — you'll have more choice. Once the colony is developed and the early-mover advantage has faded, not only are you paying a higher price for a home in the same project, you also have less to choose from. Don't be the last one to the party.

Chapter Three

Amenities & facilities in the township.

All developers advertise lifestyle benefits on their brochures. What you should be asking them about is the robustness of basic amenities — reliable water supply, power backup, a well-functioning sewage system, and waste management. That said, clubhouses, swimming pools, gyms, parks, sport facilities and proximity to shops should not be overlooked.

3.1 — Security

We will be honest: we have had a very bad experience with security companies in the past. Their guards are over-worked, working 2 to 3 shifts without a break. In our project Vardhman City, we poached our main guard from the security company and got him on our pay-roll. HD CCTV cameras are a given — we always use those at our sites. It is our ongoing endeavour to ensure your safety.

3.2 — Disaster Management

Do not overlook disaster management. We experienced it first-hand when our dragon-fruit farm caught fire. Luckily, none of our staff members were injured. Since then, we have been extra conscious regarding fire safety, emergency exits, and emergency water supply.

04
Chapter Four

Maintenance & management.

4.1 — Home Maintenance

Look at how responsive the developer is towards home maintenance and repair requests after purchase. Note that while it is easy to form an opinion on the basis of one flawed review, sometimes customer demands are unreasonable. We usually take house maintenance responsibility for the first 3 years after sale; beyond that is the customer's responsibility.

4.2 — Colony Maintenance

Find out what colony maintenance services are included. We usually cover colony cleaning, garden maintenance, security, common-area lighting and similar items. Eventually, maintenance is handed over to the society after the completion and sale of the project by the builder; then it is up to the residents to maintain the colony. Our township Vardhman City is an exception — we still do colony maintenance there despite having sold all the homes.

4.3 — Township Planning & Development Timeline

Townships are developed in phases. Beware of how this can impact your property, traffic within the township, and access to amenities. For example, traffic can get blocked because of the ongoing construction work of the next phase.

05
Chapter Five

Builder reputation & legal clearances.

Do your research thoroughly. A background check on the developer, execution of past projects and project quality (given the quality standards of construction at that time) will give you a rough idea about the developer's ability to execute and finish the project.

Ensure that the township has received all the necessary government approvals and clear land titles. You can look for the following documents on the MP RERA portal:

  1. TNCP Layout — Town & Country Planning layout.
  2. Nazul NOC & Diversion — if the project is being constructed on agricultural land and has been passed as residential in the Master Plan.
  3. Nagar Nigam Development Permission.
  4. Nagar Nigam Building Permission — only after this can a developer commence construction.
  5. RERA — most of the above documents can be found on the RERA portal; ask for the RERA number from the developer.
  6. Title of the Land.
  7. Title Deed.

RERA — how to verify a project

On the RERA portal you can have a look at all the documents and do your own due diligence on the promoter regarding legal clearances. The builder has to submit a CA certificate, Engineer's certificate and actual site photographs every quarter to depict the actual progress of the project — these will give you a rough idea to predict the possibility of timely possession.

Steps to find a RERA-approved project
  1. Visit rera.mp.gov.in.
  2. From the Projects / Promoters menu, select All Projects.
  3. Search for the project name through the search box.
  4. You will find all the information about any RERA-approved project.

Chapter Six

Taxes.

6.1 — Tax Exemptions at the Time of Purchase

When you are purchasing a new house, you can avail certain tax exemptions. There are two tax regimes within which you can file your ITR. Within the new tax regime, you do NOT get any deduction. However, within the old tax regime, you can get deductions on:

  • Interest component, under Section 24B.
  • Principal repayment, under Section 80C.
  • Stamp duty and registration charges, under Section 80C.
Heads up — capping

There is a cap of ₹2 lakh for interest under Section 24B for self-occupied property. Under Section 80C you get deduction on a lot of things including principal repayment, but the total cap under 80C is ₹1.5 lakh.

6.2 — Capital Gains Tax at the Time of Sale

A house is a capital asset. Any appreciation in the value of the property at the time of sale will incur capital gains tax. If you sell the house within 2 years, the gain is short-term and is added to your income — you'll pay income tax on it according to your tax bracket.

However, if you sell the house after 2 years, then you have to pay long-term capital gains tax.

  1. Property purchased and sold before 23/07/2024 — indexation and 20% rate only. Indexation was introduced to consider the impact of inflation on the pricing of property. Cost of acquisition × indexation factor = indexed cost of acquisition. (Selling price − indexed cost − transfer expenses) is taxed at a flat 20%.
  2. Property purchased before 23/07/2024 but sold after 23/07/2024 — indexation with 20%, or without indexation at 12.5% — at the option of the assessee (only for Individuals and HUF).
  3. Property purchased and sold after 23/07/2024 — without indexation, flat 12.5% only. Any property purchased after 23 July 2024 cannot avail indexation.

Furthermore, if your income is below the basic exemption limit, your short/long-term gain is decreased by the amount by which you fall short of the basic exemption limit. And if you reinvest the amount earned from sale of a house into specified assets like another house or REC bonds, capital gains is exempt.

Chapter Seven

Home & commercial loans.

7.1 — Home Loan

To give you a home loan, banks check your nominal minimum income eligibility. Banks provide home loan on the basis of:

  • The income generated from your business, or
  • The salary earned from your job.

7.1.1 Salaried Individuals

For banks, 60–65% of your salary can be considered for EMI. For example, if your salary is ₹1 lakh per month and you have 0 liability, banks can give up to ₹60–65 lakh as loan.

The second important factor banks consider is age, which decides the duration of the loan. If your pension is assured by your employer or the government, you may get a loan up to 70 years of age. Example: you are 40 and can assure the bank you will receive a pension — banks can give a 30-year loan until you are 70. However, if you work in a multinational without a pension provision, the cut-off age is generally 60. So at 40 you'd get a 20-year loan.

7.1.1.1 Documents required for a salaried person

  • Last 3 months' salary slips.
  • Last 2–3 years of Income Tax Returns and Form 16.
  • Last 6 months' bank statement of the salary account.

7.1.2 Business Owners

For business owners, loan eligibility is calculated on the basis of the average income of the last 3 years. Any existing loan liability is deducted and interest is added back to the aggregate income to determine eligibility. Loan can usually be given up to 70 years of age — a 40-year-old with a strong balance sheet showing decent income over the last 3 years can get a 30-year loan.

7.1.2.1 Documents required for a business owner

  • Last 3 years' ITR.
  • Last 3 years' 26AS.
  • Gumasta.
  • GST certificate.
  • 6 months' bank statement.

If the person fulfils the income eligibility criteria, they can avail a home loan of up to 75–80% of the property value. If the property value is upwards of ₹1 cr, up to 75%; under ₹1 cr, up to 80%. Another way to increase the loan amount to approximately 90% is by including the cost of interiors in the estimate.

7.2 — Under what circumstances can banks deny you a home loan?

  • CIBIL score less than 700.
  • Any unpaid dues on existing loans.
  • You haven't paid any of your installments regularly.
  • You have defaulted on credit card payments.
  • You have defaulted on a personal loan or car/two-wheeler loan.

7.2.1 How to improve your CIBIL score?

Get a credit card or a consumer loan and make regular, on-time payments of your dues for the next 4 months. This will improve your CIBIL score.

7.2.2 How should a customer decide which bank to go to?

The rate of interest for all banks is more or less the same — all banks are under RBI governance and its Repo rate.

If you want to go to a private bank, HDFC is good; for nationalised banks, Bank of Baroda and SBI are okay. However, we are of the opinion that you should use NBFCs (Non-Banking Finance Companies) primarily because they specialise in home loans — for banks, home loan is just another vertical. For example, LIC HFL is an NBFC and has mastery over distributing home loans; service is usually good. Banks, burdened with other work, may not be able to provide equally good service.

The best thing about LIC HFL: no hidden charges for search and valuation, nominal processing fees, no pre-payment penalty charges, no hidden costs, and good service.

7.2.3 You should ask your bank or NBFC about —

  • Pre-payment penalty charges in case you pay back the loan before time.
  • Any lock-in period.
  • Any charges if you want your loan to be taken over by another bank.
  • Processing fees.
  • Any search and valuation fees.

Rate of interest shouldn't be more than 0.25% higher than any nationalised bank.

How to increase your income eligibility?

You can increase your income eligibility by adding potential income from parental property, or income earned by your wife, son or daughter.

7.3 — Commercial Loans

Mortgage loan for a commercial property can be given for up to 70–75% of the property. The rate of interest for a commercial loan is 1.25% higher than a home loan. Stamp duty for home loans is 0.25% but for commercial loans 0.5%, and processing fees are usually double. The maximum term for a commercial loan is 15 years; with a good profile and special permissions, up to 20 years.

7.3.1 Which banks can entertain weak CIBIL profile?

Private players like Hinduja Home Finance, Shriram Housing Finance, Aadhar Housing Finance, SMFG Grihashakti Home Finance, Capri etc. can entertain people with weak CIBIL profile, but they charge approximately 1.25% higher than other banks.
