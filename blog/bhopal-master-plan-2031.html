Bhopal Master Plan 2031.

The single document every Bhopal home-buyer should read. Land use zones, planned roads, and where the next decade of appreciation will concentrate.

Every smart Bhopal real estate decision we have made over thirty years has started with one habit: read the master plan before signing anything. The Bhopal Master Plan 2031, prepared by the Madhya Pradesh Town & Country Planning Department, tells you exactly what the city will look like in 10 years — what's residential, what's industrial, where the new roads go, which areas the government is investing public capital in. That's where appreciation concentrates.

Here is the buyer-focused summary. We have linked the official sources where applicable so you can verify.

What the Master Plan actually is

It is a statutory document — meaning it has legal force, not just recommendation status. The plan defines:

  • Land use zones — residential, commercial, mixed use, industrial, green/recreational
  • Road network — existing and planned roads with widths
  • Water supply and drainage plan
  • Heritage and green belt zones
  • Growth corridors the government will fund infrastructure for
  • Industrial estate locations

Official access: mpurban.gov.in for the document. Interactive map: Bhopal Municipal Corporation portal.

Why this matters before you buy

  1. Land use lock. If your dream flat sits on land marked "industrial" in the master plan, the project may face approval problems and the neighborhood will get factories not parks. Check before booking.
  2. Future road widening. The plan marks future road widths. If your building's setback isn't compliant, you may lose parking, garden, or boundary wall to road expansion.
  3. Upcoming infrastructure. The plan lists planned hospitals, schools, fire stations, bus terminals. Properties within 2 km of upcoming infrastructure appreciate fastest.
  4. Population projection. The plan estimates how many people will live in each zone by 2031. Higher projected density = more retail, more amenities, more appreciation.

Three corridors the plan flags hardest

1. Ayodhya Bypass & the Outer Ring Road

The plan designates the Ayodhya Bypass corridor as primary residential expansion zone. Three specific catalysts:

  • Outer Ring Road interchange — under construction, opening 2027. Will connect Ayodhya Bypass to the airport and AIIMS Bhopal in under 22 minutes.
  • BRTS extension proposed along the spine
  • Township zoning released in 2017 for 600+ acres — already absorbed by 11 active townships

For details on the townships, see our complete Ayodhya Bypass township guide.

2. Hoshangabad Road extension toward Mandideep

The plan extends Hoshangabad Road's premium residential designation south toward the Mandideep industrial belt — but with strict green belt rules around water bodies. The catalyst here is the planned six-lane highway upgrade with service roads, designed to integrate Mandideep (a major employment hub) with Bhopal residential.

For families whose primary earners work at Mandideep or in pharma/auto, the south Hoshangabad Road belt is the strategic pick.

3. Bhauri / Kerwa Dam belt around AIIMS Bhopal

AIIMS Bhopal opened in 2018 and now employs 4,000+ people, with 6,000+ family members. The plan designates a 5 km radius around AIIMS as institutional + residential mixed. Multiple educational institutions and hospitals are following AIIMS into the belt. Expect 12-15% annual appreciation in the next 5 years here, similar to what we saw on Hoshangabad Road from 2010-2015.

Three corridors the plan does NOT flag

Equally important — areas where the plan does not direct public investment:

  • Old city core — heritage zone, density caps, limited construction approval
  • Berasia Road belt — primarily industrial designation, residential pockets exist but with mixed-use risk
  • Hills around Bhopal Lake — green/recreational designation, no new residential construction

Buying in these zones for residential investment is fine if you understand it's a coupon-style hold (steady but slow). For appreciation, go where the plan directs capital.

Three things to check before booking

  1. Is the project on residential-designated land? Open the master plan map for your survey number. The land must be marked R1, R2, or R3 (residential categories).
  2. Is the road in front of the project a "future ROW" road? If yes, find out the planned width. If it's wider than the current road, the building's boundary will move back when widening happens.
  3. What's the nearest planned amenity? The plan marks proposed schools, hospitals, parks. Within 2 km of a planned amenity = future appreciation premium.

FAQ

What is the Bhopal Master Plan 2031?

Statutory MP government document defining Bhopal's 10-year growth — land use, roads, water, amenities.

Where can I read it?

mpurban.gov.in for the document. Bhopal Municipal Corporation portal for the interactive map.

How does it affect prices?

Residential-designated areas with planned infrastructure appreciate 14-18% annually. Other zones flatter.

Which corridors will benefit most?

Ayodhya Bypass (ORR interchange), Hoshangabad Road south (Mandideep highway), Bhauri/Kerwa (AIIMS belt).
